
# Problem

Imagine that you're in a boat. you can paddle with 3m/s in still water. When you have to cross the river in which direction relative to the bank should you row in order to cross from one bank to the other along the shortest possible route? The river current has the same sepeed everrywhere:

a) 2m/s
b) 4m/s



# Pause and think

First let's consider the case when the river's speed is 2m/s. Pause the video and think about it a little bit. It clearly doesn't seem like a good idea to row in the direction of the sream. If you row against the stream you end up not moving away from the bank. You might think that rowing perpendicular to the bank would be the optimal path. And indeed it is the fastest way to get to the oter side. But it's not the shortest.

We are aiming for the shortest path, so -- if it's possible -- then the best route is to move perpendicular to the coast. But the problem is that you have to account for the rivers velocity as well. This is why simply rowing straight across the river is not the right option.

# Solution

Let's look at part a) first:

The good news is that we can perform simple vector addition to get the net velocity. So as we discussed earlyer we want the net velocity to be perpendicular to the bank. Now our only task is that we somehow have to reverse-engineer in which direction we have to paddle.

And now we're gonna be a little tricky here. We first draw the rivers velocity relative to the bank. We can forget abut the fact that this vectors hase the unit in m/s. So just draw a vector with the lengt of two, pointing downstream. Then around the tip of the rivers velocity vector we draw a circle with radius 3. Now, because the only constraint is that our speed -- relative to the river -- has to bee 3m/s, our velocity vector can land on any points on this circle, starting from the center of the circle. This vector represents the our velocity relative to the river.

Now, remember that we get the velocity that is observed from the ground, by adding the rivers velocity to our velocity that is relative to the river.  Great, so this net velocity is starting from the tail of the river's velocity vector and it ends at the tip of our velocity vector relative to the river. As a last piece of the solution we have to recall that we want the net velocity to point perpendicular to the bank. So we can move around the circle to get that favorable position.

By doing that the roblem reduces to a simple trigonometry problem. $$cos(\alpha) = 2/3 $$ $$\alpha \approx 48 ^\circ$$
