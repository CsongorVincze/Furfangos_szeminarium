
# Problem F.6

From one side of a straight channel of constant width, a boatman sets off toward the point directly opposite on the other side. The water in the channel is moving everywhere at speed $v$. The boatman rows at a constant rate, so that in still water he would also travel at speed $v$. He always points his boat toward the destination point, so the water carries him downstream. Fortunately, he never gets tired.

How far downstream does the current carry the boatman? Where does he arrive? What path does he follow relative to the bank?


# Pause and think

When I tackle a problem like this I like to draw first to build some intuition. So I encourage you too to sit down and try to draw the path of the boat, or least think it trough in your head how it might look like.

For this problem the key to the key to the solution is first answering the last question. What path does the boat follow. Once we figured this out we'll get the answers for the other two questions for free.

So first let's draw. Although the direction of the boat's velocity is changing we can say that for a short amount of time it does not really rotate. so with this method we can draw the trajectory.

One thing that you might observe is that the net velocity is always tangential to the actual path, the rivers velocity is pointing always downward and the the boatman's paddling direction always points towards one fixed point, no matter what.

This property might ring some bells.
